# The case register of the resolution algorithm

## Status and rules of this register

This register is permanent content of this repo. It is
the accounting that the algorithm specified in
`algorithm.md` is caseless in the sense of its
section 17: every input shape the argument of that
spec enumerates is entered here with what the
algorithm does on it, the rule and claim that cover
it, and its state.

The register is append only. Entries are never edited
in place, never renumbered, and never removed. An
entry changes state only by a dated state line
appended at the end of the entry, under `algorithm.md`
section 17.2. A new case is appended under
section 17.2 of that spec: the next number in its
family, or a new family appended at the end with the
next letter, its dimension named in its heading.

Four states are kept distinct throughout, as in the
whole program: settled intent, proposed semantics,
implemented behavior, verified behavior. A case state
here is one of two words. Accounted: a rule of
`algorithm.md` covers the case, and the covering claim
is named; the claim itself stands at the state its own
line in `algorithm.md` section 18 gives, which is
theorem to be or premise, never verified here. Gap:
the case is an open specification gap of
`algorithm.md` section 19, named with its owner, and
the algorithm follows the interim rule there.

The seed below is generated by crossing the dimensions
listed in `algorithm.md` section 17.3, and no other
source. Family letters and case numbers are labels of
this register only; they name no value of the model.

## Family A — the state of the graph

### Case A1 — empty graph, empty candidate list

Instance: the empty graph of `spec.md` section 5.3; a
target whose terms are in scope; no candidates.
Does: intake charges nothing; the target is recorded
under `algorithm.md` section 9.2, its terms admitted,
its bound taken, being the state of its terms and
their singleton edges. Output is that record alone.
Covered by: sections 8 step 4, 9.2, and 10.1; claim
eight for the bound. State: accounted.

### Case A2 — empty graph, candidates present

Instance: the empty graph; candidates naming terms
none of which is collected.
Does: join candidates cost their join plus two
admissions each, paint candidates have no cost and
defer with reason collected until a sweep in which
another candidate has admitted their term, under
sections 7.3 and 8 step 5.
Covered by: sections 7.3 and 8; claim six. State:
accounted.

### Case A3 — target term already collected, alone

Instance: a target term standing in a singleton edge
of the input graph.
Does: its admission as part of the target record
changes no edge and appends the entry, as `spec.md`
section 8.2 operation 1 provides; the bound includes
that edge whole.
Covered by: sections 9.2 and 10.1; claim one. State:
accounted.

### Case A4 — target term already collected, in a wide edge

Instance: a target term standing in an edge with
several other terms.
Does: the bound taken is that whole edge's state,
under `spec.md` section 7.2 saturation; the view takes
the edge whole. No term of the edge is treated as
outside the bound.
Covered by: sections 10.1 and 11.1; claims eight and
nine. State: accounted.

### Case A5 — one term collected under two levels

Instance: a term named by the target level and by a
level already recorded in the input graph.
Does: the term stands in exactly one edge, by the
partition invariant of `spec.md` section 5.2; no
reconciliation step runs, under `spec.md` section 7.4
clause 2 and `algorithm.md` section 9.3.
Covered by: section 9.3; claim seven. State:
accounted.

### Case A6 — a graph value that is not valid

Instance: an input value failing a validity invariant
of `spec.md` section 5.2.
Does: the run does not start; the result record is
the single line graph not valid; nothing is repaired
and nothing is appended, under section 8 step 0.
Covered by: sections 8 step 0 and 16.1. State:
accounted.

## Family B — kind and collection state of a candidate

### Case B1 — admit a new term

Instance: an admit candidate whose term is in scope,
relevant, and not collected.
Does: one operation, charged one step, no witness
check; a singleton edge is appended.
Covered by: sections 7.1, 7.3, and 8 step 5. State:
accounted.

### Case B2 — admit a term already collected

Instance: an admit candidate whose term is collected.
Does: the operation changes no edge and appends its
entry, charged one step; this is repeat witnessing,
and it is not redundancy, under `spec.md` sections
8.2 and 10.
Covered by: sections 7.3 and 8 step 5. State:
accounted.

### Case B3 — join two uncollected terms

Instance: a join candidate, both terms in scope and
uncollected, witness passing.
Does: charged four steps as one atomic unit, two
admissions, the join, and its witness check, counted
as section 7.3 provides; the terms are admitted in
that unit and then merged into one edge.
Covered by: sections 7.3, 7.5, and 8 step 5; claim
one. State: accounted.

### Case B4 — join with one term collected

Instance: a join candidate, one term collected, one
not, witness passing.
Does: the uncollected term is admitted as part of the
join step and the two edges merge into one, under
`spec.md` section 8.2 operation 2.
Covered by: sections 7.3 and 8 step 5. State:
accounted.

### Case B5 — join two terms in two edges

Instance: a join candidate whose terms stand in two
distinct edges, witness passing.
Does: the two edges are replaced by one edge holding
both term lists and the union of both color lists, as
`spec.md` section 8.2 operation 2 provides; excess
rises by exactly one, by `spec.md` section 13 claim
one.
Covered by: section 8 step 5; claim one. State:
accounted.

### Case B6 — join two terms already in one edge

Instance: a join candidate whose terms already stand
together, witness passing.
Does: no edge change; the entry is appended; charged
the join and its check. Co-membership already in the
graph needed no candidate, but a presented candidate
is processed as presented.
Covered by: sections 7.3 and 8 step 5. State:
accounted.

### Case B7 — join a term to itself

Instance: a join candidate whose two terms are the
same term by spelling.
Does: applied as `spec.md` section 8.2 operation 2
provides, marked resolved with the note self, and
expressly not a witness of redundancy, since
redundancy requires two distinct spellings in one
edge.
Covered by: section 8 step 5. State: accounted.

### Case B8 — record a level not yet recorded

Instance: a record candidate or the target, its name
not recorded, all its terms in the effective scope.
Does: terms admitted first, the level value appended,
the bound taken and recorded at that moment, charged
atomically under sections 7.5 and 9.2.
Covered by: sections 9.2 and 10.1; claim eight. State:
accounted.

### Case B9 — record a level already recorded, same content

Instance: a level of the same name recorded with the
same terms and the same scope.
Does: repeat witnessing; only the log entry is
appended, with the bound taken at this moment recorded
in it, as `spec.md` section 8.2 operation 3 provides;
no second level value stands.
Covered by: section 9.2. State: accounted.

### Case B10 — record a level name with different content

Instance: a level of the same name recorded with
different terms or a different scope than the
candidate presents.
Does: refused with reason name; nothing appended.
Covered by: section 9.2, as the interim rule of gap
one. State: gap — gap one of `algorithm.md`
section 19; owner, the coordinator.

### Case B11 — paint a new color

Instance: a paint candidate whose term is collected,
its color not on the edge, witness passing.
Does: the color is appended to the edge's color list;
the entry is appended; charged paint and check.
Covered by: sections 7.3 and 12.1; claim ten. State:
accounted.

### Case B12 — paint a color already present

Instance: a paint candidate whose color, in all four
fields of `spec.md` section 9.1, is already on the
edge.
Does: the color list does not change, since it holds
no repeated member; the painting entry is appended
either way, as `spec.md` section 8.2 operation 4
provides. The same painter painting the same mark at
a later epoch is a different color, by epoch, and is
case B11, not this case.
Covered by: section 12.1; claim ten. State:
accounted.

### Case B13 — paint a term not collected

Instance: a paint candidate whose term is not
collected, and no candidate in the run admits it.
Does: the candidate has no cost at that graph and is
deferred with reason collected; the algorithm invents
no admission to supply the term, under section 7.3.
Covered by: sections 7.3 and 8 step 5; claim six.
State: accounted.

## Family C — the scope state of terms

### Case C1 — a trivial term under any ceiling

Instance: an atom, or a form with an atom head and
atom arguments only, under `spec.md` section 3.1.
Does: in the effective scope under every ceiling; its
required rank is trivial, so it is visited first under
section 6.
Covered by: sections 5.1 and 6. State: accounted.

### Case C2 — a first order term under a trivial ceiling

Instance: a form with an atom head and a compound
argument, the ceiling being the trivial scope.
Does: outside the effective scope; every candidate
naming it as a term to resolve is deferred with reason
scope; the term is absent from the output graph unless
already collected, and its state is unmeasured.
Covered by: sections 5.1 and 16.4; claim three. State:
accounted.

### Case C3 — a higher order term under a first order ceiling

Instance: a form whose head is itself a form, anywhere
in its tree; the ceiling being the first order scope.
Does: as case C2, with reason scope. A coarser reading
of the behavior, presented as its own term in scope,
may be resolved as itself, never as a substitute for
this term, under section 5.1.
Covered by: sections 5.1 and 16.4; claim three. State:
accounted.

### Case C4 — the scope admits nothing presented

Instance: an effective scope under which no candidate
term and no target term passes, whether because the
ceiling is narrow or because an appended predicate
passes no presented term.
Does: the run resolves nothing; the target record is
deferred whole with reason scope where it has terms;
the result record reports every line deferred; budget
is spent only on intake tests. This is the empty
scope boundary, and it is a normal run.
Covered by: sections 5.1, 8 steps 1 and 4, and 16.
State: accounted.

### Case C5 — a level with an empty term list

Instance: a target or record candidate whose term
list is empty.
Does: it is recorded as a level with no terms; it
admits nothing; its bound is the empty state; its view
is the empty list. It is not case C4: the scope is not
empty, the level is.
Covered by: section 9.2. State: accounted.

### Case C6 — one term of a record outside the scope

Instance: a record whose term list holds one term
outside the effective scope and the rest inside.
Does: the record is deferred whole with reason scope;
none of its admissions is applied as part of the
record, under the atomicity of section 8 step 4.
Separate admit candidates for the in scope terms are
unaffected.
Covered by: sections 8 step 4 and 9.2. State:
accounted.

### Case C7 — an appended scope with a declared rank

Instance: a scope predicate appended under `spec.md`
section 3.1, carrying a declared rank as section 5.2
requires.
Does: the effective scope and the visiting order use
the declared rank exactly as a named scope's rank is
used.
Covered by: sections 5.2 and 6. State: accounted.

### Case C8 — an appended scope with no declared rank

Instance: an appended scope predicate with no rank in
the input.
Does: work under it is deferred with reason rank;
nothing under it is resolved in the run.
Covered by: section 5.2, as the interim rule of gap
two. State: gap — gap two of `algorithm.md`
section 19; owner, the coordinator.

### Case C9 — widening between runs

Instance: a term deferred for scope in one run, then
presented again in a later run with a wider ceiling.
Does: the later run resolves it if its other tests
pass; the earlier run's appends stand unchanged and
the growth relation holds across the widening, under
section 5.3.
Covered by: section 5.3; claim one. State: accounted.

## Family D — the witness state

### Case D1 — witness present, check passes

Instance: a join, paint, or record candidate whose
witness names a proof, a derivation in a derived
view, or a measurement at a named pin, and whose check
passes.
Does: the operation is applied on that witness, which
is recorded in the entry, as `spec.md` section 6
requires of equivalence assertions.
Covered by: sections 4.1 and 8 step 5. State:
accounted.

### Case D2 — no witness presented

Instance: a join, paint, or record candidate whose
witness field is the fixed empty name symbol.
Does: refused with reason witness; nothing is
appended for it; the check step is not charged, since
no check is run on an absent witness, the absence
being read at intake and at the visit.
Covered by: sections 4.1 and 16.1. State: accounted.

### Case D3 — witness check fails

Instance: a candidate whose witness is presented and
whose check returns fail.
Does: refused with reason witness; the check step is
charged; the operation part of its cost is never
charged and nothing is applied, under section 8
step 5.
Covered by: section 8 step 5; premise two covers the
honesty of a pass, not of a fail. State: accounted.

### Case D4 — the same witness for two candidates

Instance: two candidates in one run naming the same
witness ground for different changes.
Does: each candidate is checked on its own pair of
candidate and witness under section 7.2; a pass for
one is not a pass for the other, since the pairs
differ. A second presentation of the same candidate,
in all five fields, takes the cached verdict at no
check charge.
Covered by: section 7.2. State: accounted.

### Case D5 — equivalence offered from spelling, a name, or a hash

Instance: a join candidate whose named ground is the
spelling of its terms, an identifier, or a hash of
their content.
Does: refused with reason witness. `spec.md`
section 6 forbids equivalence from each of these
grounds, and the algorithm applies that rule at the
visit, whatever the check input returns.
Covered by: sections 11.1 and 16.1. State: accounted.

### Case D6 — two spellings differing only in argument order

Instance: two forms whose argument lists differ only
in order, with no join candidate between them.
Does: nothing joins them; they stand in distinct
edges until a join candidate on a witness joins them,
as `spec.md` section 2.2 and its control order
provide. The algorithm does not silently identify
them at intake, at any sweep, or in any reading.
Covered by: section 9.3. State: accounted.

### Case D7 — a view offered as a witness

Instance: a candidate whose named ground is that its
terms stand together in a view or a filtered view.
Does: refused with reason witness, under section
11.1: a view is a reading, never evidence about its
own members.
Covered by: section 11.1. State: accounted.

## Family E — the budget state

### Case E1 — budget zero

Instance: availability with budget zero in form one.
Does: no intake test can be charged, so no candidate
is tested and none is resolved; every candidate is
deferred with reason budget, the target record is
deferred with reason budget, and the target line
states bound not taken, under section 10.4. The output
graph is the input graph.
Covered by: sections 7, 8 steps 1 and 4, and 10.4;
gap eight covers whether such a run counts as a
projection. State: accounted, with gap eight open on
the projection wording only; owner, the coordinator.

### Case E2 — budget exactly the counted cost of the run

Instance: a budget equal to the sum the run spends.
Does: the run completes every resolution its tests
pass, ending with budget left zero; no candidate is
deferred for budget.
Covered by: sections 7 and 8. State: accounted.

### Case E3 — budget short by one at a join

Instance: at a join candidate's visit, budget left is
its counted cost minus one.
Does: the join is not started, no sub step of it is
applied, and it is deferred with reason budget; the
pass continues to later candidates, which may cost
less, under section 8 step 5.
Covered by: sections 7.5 and 8 step 5; claim six.
State: accounted.

### Case E4 — exhaustion between the sub steps a join would need

Instance: a join whose two terms are uncollected, in
a run whose budget could pay one admission but not
the whole counted cost of the join.
Does: the join is not started and neither admission
is applied as part of it, under section 7.5. Where an
earlier candidate has already admitted one of the
terms on its own account, that admission stands as
growth, and the join's cost is counted again at its
visit against the graph as it then stands.
Covered by: section 7.5. State: accounted.

### Case E5 — exhaustion before the target record

Instance: intake charges leave less than the target
record's counted cost.
Does: the target is deferred whole with its reason;
bound not taken is stated; candidates are still
visited and may resolve, their appends being growth
with or without the record, under section 8 step 4.
Covered by: sections 8 step 4 and 10.4; gap eight
covers the projection wording. State: accounted, with
gap eight open on the projection wording only; owner,
the coordinator.

### Case E6 — exhaustion in the middle of a list of paints

Instance: several paint candidates on one edge, the
budget paying for some and not the rest.
Does: paints are separate candidates; those visited
while the budget pays them resolve in visiting order,
and the rest defer with reason budget. No paint is
half applied, a color being appended whole or not at
all.
Covered by: sections 7.5, 8 step 5, and 12.1. State:
accounted.

### Case E7 — budget left, every remaining candidate costing more

Instance: at the stop, budget left is positive and
every deferred candidate's counted cost at the output
graph exceeds it.
Does: the run ends with the budget left reported;
the left budget is not waste and is not carried to a
later run, under sections 16.2 and 16.3.
Covered by: sections 16.2 and 16.3; claims six and
thirteen. State: accounted.

### Case E8 — budget in the full form

Instance: availability in form two of section 4.2,
the word full.
Does: the budget is fixed at intake as the dry count
of section 7.4; the run proceeds exactly as a form one
run with that budget, and may end with budget left,
which is reported and is not an error.
Covered by: sections 4.2 and 7.4. State: accounted.

### Case E9 — availability in a form that is not counted

Instance: availability presented as a time, a price,
a venue, or a rate.
Does: the run does not start under that availability;
the algorithm does not translate it, under gap three.
The caller supplies the counted form.
Covered by: section 4.2, as the interim rule of gap
three. State: gap — gap three of `algorithm.md`
section 19; owners, the coordinator with the
measurement lane.

## Family F — the level and bound state

### Case F1 — the target is already resolved

Instance: the target is recorded with the same terms
and scope, every candidate presented is already
effected in the input graph, and the bound stands
recorded from its earlier moment.
Does: with an empty candidate list the run appends
nothing and reports the target resolved already, its
bound being the earlier recorded bound, never retaken.
With repeated candidates, each is processed as the
repeat its operation defines, under family B.
Covered by: sections 9.2 and 10.1. State: accounted.

### Case F2 — bound reached

Instance: a full pass in which no step admits a term
into an edge the bound reading meets and no join
merges such an edge.
Does: the bound reading has stopped growing; the run
stops at the sweep condition, candidates outside the
bound reading and the derived set defer with reason
target, and claims outside the recorded bound are
unmeasured, under section 10.3.
Covered by: sections 10.3 and 16.4. State: accounted.

### Case F3 — a candidate joining a term outside the bound

Instance: a join candidate with one term in the bound
reading and one term outside it, in scope, witness
passing.
Does: the candidate is relevant by the join clause of
section 8 step 3; on resolution the outside term is
captured into the bound reading, the recorded bound
standing unchanged as the record of its moment.
Covered by: sections 8 step 3 and 10.2. State:
accounted.

### Case F4 — two levels appended in either order

Instance: the target level and one further level,
appended by two runs in one order, and by two runs in
the other order, from the same starting graph.
Does: the final partitions and color sets agree up to
state equivalence, the recorded bounds are as taken at
their moments, and growth holds from the start in both
orders.
Covered by: section 9.3; claim seven. State:
accounted.

### Case F5 — a finer term that is a part of a coarser target term

Instance: a candidate naming a term that is a part,
at any depth, of a term of the target, the finer term
itself in scope.
Does: the candidate is relevant by the parts clause
of section 8 step 3 and is resolved on its own tests.
The algorithm invents no finer term by expanding
parts on its own, under section 9.3.
Covered by: sections 8 step 3 and 9.3. State:
accounted.

### Case F6 — a finer term not presented

Instance: a coarser target term whose parts include
terms no candidate, target, or intent item names.
Does: those parts are not admitted, not derived, and
not reported as resolved; parts descent is a reading
over presented terms only, and it ends, under
section 9.3.
Covered by: section 9.3; section 16.4 for their state.
State: accounted.

### Case F7 — the bound reading grows after the bound is recorded

Instance: joins after the target record merge new
terms into edges the target's terms stand in.
Does: the bound reading at the running graph grows;
the recorded bound does not change, is not recomputed
as a record, and is not restated, under sections 10.1
and 10.2, the record and the reading kept apart by
name.
Covered by: sections 10.1 and 10.2; claim eight.
State: accounted.

### Case F8 — a target whose terms stand in no edge at its record

Instance: every target term uncollected at step 4.
Does: the record admits them all first, each in its
own singleton edge, and the bound is the state of
those singleton edges joined with any edge a term
already stood in, there being none; the view at the
target is those edges whole.
Covered by: sections 9.2, 10.1, and 11.1. State:
accounted.

## Family G — the view and filter state

### Case G1 — the view is empty

Instance: no edge of the graph meets the target's
bound, as where the target was never recorded and no
term of it is collected.
Does: the view is the empty list, a reading of no
edges; nothing is deferred or refused by the view
itself.
Covered by: section 11.1. State: accounted.

### Case G2 — a filter passes no edge

Instance: a filtered view whose predicate passes no
color of any edge of the view.
Does: the filtered view is empty as a reading; it
paints nothing, defers nothing, and refuses nothing,
under section 11.2.
Covered by: section 11.2. State: accounted.

### Case G3 — a filter passes some edges

Instance: a filtered view whose predicate passes
colors on some edges of the view and not others.
Does: the filtered view lists the passing edges
whole, all their terms and all their colors, including
colors the predicate does not pass; filtering never
selects part of an edge.
Covered by: section 11.2. State: accounted.

### Case G4 — the observed filter read as the qualified filter

Instance: a reading or a candidate that treats what
an environment makes visible as what is both intended
and observed, under `spec.md` section 9.2.
Does: as a reading, a filter is applied as written
and yields what it yields. As a ground for painting
the intended mark, it fails the two filter rule and
the paint defers with reason filter, under sections
11.2 and 12.3. The two filters are never conflated by
the algorithm.
Covered by: sections 11.2 and 12.3; claim ten. State:
accounted.

### Case G5 — a view under growth

Instance: the view at the target taken at the input
graph and again at the output graph.
Does: every edge of the first view is a subset, in
terms and in colors, of exactly one edge of the
second view, under section 11.1.
Covered by: section 11.1; claim nine. State:
accounted.

### Case G6 — a filter predicate in no named language

Instance: a filter supplied as a decidable predicate
value, its statement language being no scheme of
`spec.md` section 9.
Does: the filter is used as a reading only, exactly
as supplied, under section 11.2.
Covered by: section 11.2, as the interim rule of gap
seven. State: gap — gap seven of `algorithm.md`
section 19; owner, the coloring and qualification
lane.

## Family H — the qualification overlap state

### Case H1 — two qualifications paint one edge, different marks

Instance: two paint candidates of different painters,
or one painter at two epochs, painting different
marks on the same edge.
Does: both colors stand on the edge as distinct
entries; there is no priority, no single color of the
edge, and no overwriting, under section 12.1.
Covered by: section 12.1; claim ten. State:
accounted.

### Case H2 — one qualification paints intended, another paints stray, on one edge

Instance: the same edge painted intended by one
qualification and stray by another.
Does: both marks stand; neither shadows the other;
any reading that needs one qualification's mark reads
that painter's entries, under section 12.1.
Covered by: section 12.1; claim ten. State:
accounted.

### Case H3 — a qualification applied by appending

Instance: a qualification that resolves new terms, a
new equivalence, or a new level, painting its colors
as it goes.
Does: its candidates are ordinary candidates of
family B, each on its own witness, with the
qualification as painter where the operation paints,
under section 12.1 and `spec.md` section 9.2 clause 1.
Covered by: section 12.1. State: accounted.

### Case H4 — an edge spanning two levels, painted at one

Instance: an edge meeting the bounds of two levels; a
paint candidate naming a term of it at one level.
Does: the edge is painted whole, and the painting is
visible in the views of both levels; the edge is
never split or copied by level, under section 12.2.
Covered by: section 12.2. State: accounted.

### Case H5 — the intended mark where both entries stand

Instance: a paint of the intended mark of a
qualification on an edge holding both an observation
entry and an intent entry of that qualification, the
paint's own witness passing.
Does: the mark is painted, under section 12.3.
Covered by: section 12.3; claim ten. State:
accounted.

### Case H6 — the intended mark where only one entry stands

Instance: as case H5, but only the observation entry
stands, or only the intent entry stands, at the
candidate's visit.
Does: deferred with reason filter; it may pass in a
later sweep if the missing entry is painted meanwhile
on its own witness; at the end of the run, no intended
mark stands from this run on that edge.
Covered by: section 12.3; claim ten. State:
accounted.

## Family I — intent and new information

### Case I1 — an intent term already collected, in a wide edge

Instance: an intent item whose term stands in an edge
with other terms.
Does: the whole edge enters the overlap source of the
derived set; every term of the edge is a member of the
derived set, under section 13.1 source three.
Covered by: section 13.1; claim eleven. State:
accounted.

### Case I2 — an intent term not yet captured

Instance: an intent item whose term is not collected
in the graph.
Does: the term enters the stated source as spelled;
it is a member of the derived set; it is admitted
only by a candidate or the target on the usual tests,
and it is equivalent to nothing by intent alone,
under sections 13.1 and 13.3.
Covered by: sections 13.1 and 13.3; claims eleven and
twelve. State: accounted.

### Case I3 — new information joining a collected term on a witness

Instance: a join candidate joining a term not
collected to a term in the bound reading, witness
passing, the new term in scope.
Does: the new term is admitted as part of the join
and merged; at the next reading of the derived set it
stands in the overlap source, its edge being the
merged edge, under sections 8 step 6 and 13.3.
Covered by: sections 13.2 and 13.3; claim eleven.
State: accounted.

### Case I4 — intent overlapping a filtered view

Instance: an intent term standing in an edge of a
filtered view at the target, the edge holding terms
beyond the intent term.
Does: those terms enter the derived set by the
overlap source as members of that collected edge; the
filter is a reading that selected the edge, and it is
evidence for nothing, under sections 11.2 and 13.1.
Covered by: sections 13.1 and 13.3; claim twelve.
State: accounted.

### Case I5 — intent overlapping an edge spanning levels

Instance: an intent term in an edge that meets the
bounds of two levels.
Does: the whole edge enters the overlap source once;
the derived set holds its terms once, read up to
membership, whichever level the reading is taken at,
under sections 12.2 and 13.2.
Covered by: section 13.2; claim eleven. State:
accounted.

### Case I6 — two intents, two painters, one term
